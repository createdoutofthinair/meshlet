# Procedural planet terrain: research and implementation

This lab explores seeded, texture-free terrain on a planet. The references below
are primary research or documentation from the authors. Their algorithms are
research inputs, not a claim that the lab implements every result.

## Terrain formation and detail

- [A Review of Digital Terrain Modeling](https://diglib.eg.org/items/47f03175-46e8-44e8-94ef-663bef354d1c) (Galin et al., 2019) surveys procedural, simulation and example-based methods, and explains why homogeneous fractal noise alone produces weak landform structure. **Applied here:** distinct continental, mountain-belt and detail scales, with a land mask and warped 3D noise.
- [Generation of Folded Terrains from Simple Vector Maps](https://diglib.eg.org/items/5ecd72bf-b3a5-46be-b108-54e556b23f99) (Michel et al., 2015) explains the importance of connected mountain ranges and tectonic folds. **Applied here:** seeded spherical plate-center controls and a smooth boundary mask localize uplift. This is a procedural approximation; no plate motion is simulated.
- [Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion](https://diglib.eg.org/items/13e52c36-0200-4652-aacf-17aa3098c5fd) (Cordonnier et al., 2016) combines uplift, stream networks and erosion. **Applied here:** a coarse, single-flow drainage graph and an artist-tuned stream-power-style erosion step. This is an independently written approximation, not their complete uplift and erosion model.
- [Physically-based Analytical Erosion for Fast Terrain Generation](https://diglib.eg.org/items/0a412929-2e29-4bcb-a597-1a4b2cc558e2) (Tzathas et al., 2024) combines stream-power solutions with hillslope and landslide processes. **Future research:** evaluate it as an optional cached world-generation stage, including its computational cost and license.
- [Real-time Terrain Enhancement with Controlled Procedural Patterns](https://diglib.eg.org/items/43319e3d-ba25-4762-971b-ac838ec21fb8) (Grenier et al., 2024) uses terrain slope to orient cascaded ravine patterns. **Applied here:** the general principle of slope-guided detail, using our own analytic value-noise gradients and directional filtering. This is not their phasor-noise algorithm; no paper code is included. The paper is published under a non-commercial license.
- [Terrain Amplification using Multi-Scale Erosion](https://h-schott.github.io/p/mserosion/) (Schott et al., 2024) combines erosion and deposition processes across scales to maintain hydrological structure. **Applied here:** a cached, coarse world pass for drainage and slope-dependent sediment deposition alongside procedural detail. The multi-scale erosion method itself is not implemented; the fine detail is not hydrologically guaranteed.
- [FastFlow: GPU Acceleration of Flow and Depression Routing for Landscape Simulation](https://diglib.eg.org/items/5c046b92-a474-429d-9d0f-c028b55eef36) (Jain et al., 2024) motivates GPU flow accumulation and depression routing. **Applied here:** a separate, simpler fixed-iteration relaxation and gather pass; this is not FastFlow and does not inherit its convergence or performance guarantees.
- [r.watershed](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass85/manuals/r.watershed.html) documents single- and multiple-flow-direction watershed analysis. **Applied here:** one downhill neighbor per node on a depression-filled surface; unlike a full watershed model, the result is a coarse rendering approximation.
- [Generating Complex Procedural Terrains Using the GPU](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu) (NVIDIA GPU Gems 3) describes multi-scale procedural fields and domain warping. **Applied here:** low-frequency 3D domain warp and bounded higher-frequency relief bands. The planet is a heightfield, not a volumetric cave system.

## Planet-scale rendering

- [Geometry Clipmaps](https://hhoppe.com/proj/geomclipmap/) (Losasso and Hoppe, 2004) and [GPU-based Geometry Clipmaps](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-i-geometric-complexity/chapter-2-terrain-rendering-using-gpu-based-geometry) demonstrate nested grids and incremental GPU terrain updates. **Applied here:** reusable regular grids and GPU-side terrain generation; this lab uses a cube-sphere quadtree rather than a clipmap.
- [3D Tiles selection and screen-space error](https://cesium.com/learn/cesium-native/ref-doc/selection-algorithm-details.html) shows refinement based on estimated geometric error. **Current implementation:** a projected grid-cell-size heuristic with a hard patch budget, not an error-bounded SSE implementation. A conservative per-node geometric error is future work.
- [Horizon culling](https://cesium.com/blog/2013/04/25/horizon-culling/) discusses avoiding false culls when terrain crosses an idealized ellipsoid. **Applied here:** CPU-side conservative horizon rejection uses an inner and outer radius with a margin for bounded displacement.

## Implemented pipeline

1. Six consistently oriented cube faces form a sphere with an Earth-like mean radius of 6,371 km. A CPU quadtree selects patches within a user-controlled budget; levels can reach 20 when the camera and budget permit. Adjacent leaves are balanced to a maximum level difference of one, including across cube faces. Previously split nodes receive a small priority margin on subsequent selections to reduce patch churn. This is spatial hysteresis, not a time-based terrain fade.
2. Each 16 × 16-cell patch has 16 meshlets of 4 × 4 cells, 32 triangles per meshlet. Coarse-neighbor edge flags collapse odd edge vertices to match the adjacent grid. New patches are generated and cached on the GPU; cached normals are updated when neighboring levels change.
3. Seeded plate centers, warped continental fields, ridged multifractals, directional folds and fine relief bands form the base terrain. Analytic gradients of larger ridges orient two scales of incised valley detail. Seeded bands displace vertices from tens of meters down to sub-meter scales. This is procedural synthesis; there is no plate-motion simulation or photographic height map.
4. Once per seed, a separate GPU pass builds a world field on six 64 × 64 cube faces. The 24,578 vertices at their shared edges and corners use the same simulation data. On this coarse grid, fixed-iteration minimax relaxation approximates depression filling, one downhill neighbor is selected for each land node, and rainfall accumulates through the graph. Three passes use an artist-tuned stream-power-style rule to remove material and transport and deposit sediment. The final graph, filled elevations and erosion delta are cached. Changing the erosion slider scales that cached delta; it does not rerun the simulation for each slider position.
5. GPU-generated river splines join the drainage graph nodes; a denser coverage field is cached for each generated terrain patch and sampled during rendering. Neighboring mixed-LOD patches blend coverage at their shared edge. Rivers carve terrain near their paths, and coarse filled depressions produce approximate lakes. Moisture- and temperature-inspired color uses runoff, rain, latitude and elevation. These effects are analytical and use no image textures.
6. Compute passes calculate normals, approximate cavity shading and meshlet bounds. A compute pass frustum-culls active meshlets and writes an indirect draw. A flat ocean uses spherical normals and no seabed cavity shadow; exposing the seabed restores terrain shading. Camera-relative positions, reversed-Z depth and high/low spherical patch centers improve large-world precision. Navigation searches and a small surface probe run on the GPU.

## Current limitations

- The world field has spacing on the order of 100 km, uses one flow direction per node and fixed iteration counts, and is not a calibrated physical simulation. The procedural fine-scale terrain is not guaranteed to follow a monotonically descending coarse river profile. Some seeds or more complex terrain could exceed the fixed iteration limits. Deposition is bounded for visual stability and is not exactly mass-conserving. Lakes are a filled-surface approximation; water is not dynamically simulated.
- Climate colors are an artistic approximation, not a climate or ecosystem simulation. Rock materials, cast terrain shadows, clouds, atmospheric scattering and volumetric caves are absent; this is not photorealistic Earth.
- The quadtree uses a budget-limited projected grid-cell-size heuristic, not a conservative geometric error bound. LOD hysteresis reduces unnecessary changes, but transitions remain discrete and can pop; edge stitching is not a proof of zero pixel seams.
- Shared-edge GPU tests show sub-meter differences on coarse planet-scale patches on the test adapter. High/low sampling improves the smallest noise bands, but larger-scale fields and GPU implementation differences still limit precision; centimeter-scale guarantees need further work.
- Fine procedural bands are not fully frequency-filtered by projected sampling rate and can alias during descent. Stable band-limited residuals are future work.
- Patch selection runs on the CPU and is recomputed as the view changes. GPU geometry and river coverage are cached, but changing the seed requires world regeneration and large camera jumps can create generation bursts. Real hardware should be profiled before making performance claims.

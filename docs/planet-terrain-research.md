# Procedural planet terrain: research and implementation

This lab explores seeded, texture-free terrain on a planet. The references below
are primary research or documentation from the authors. Their algorithms are
research inputs, not a claim that the lab implements every result.

## Terrain formation and detail

- [A Review of Digital Terrain Modeling](https://diglib.eg.org/items/47f03175-46e8-44e8-94ef-663bef354d1c) (Galin et al., 2019) surveys procedural, simulation and example-based methods, and explains why homogeneous fractal noise alone produces weak landform structure. **Applied here:** distinct continental, mountain-belt and detail scales, with a land mask and warped 3D noise.
- [Generation of Folded Terrains from Simple Vector Maps](https://diglib.eg.org/items/5ecd72bf-b3a5-46be-b108-54e556b23f99) (Michel et al., 2015) explains the importance of connected mountain ranges and tectonic folds. **Applied here:** seeded spherical plate-center controls and a smooth boundary mask localize uplift. This is a procedural approximation; no plate motion is simulated.
- [Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion](https://diglib.eg.org/items/13e52c36-0200-4652-aacf-17aa3098c5fd) (Cordonnier et al., 2016) combines uplift, stream networks and erosion. **Applied here:** a coarse, single-flow drainage graph and an artist-tuned stream-power-style erosion step. This is an independently written approximation, not their complete uplift and erosion model.
- [Physically-based Analytical Erosion for Fast Terrain Generation](https://diglib.eg.org/items/0a412929-2e29-4bcb-a597-1a4b2cc558e2) (Tzathas et al., 2024) combines stream-power solutions with hillslope and landslide processes. **Future research:** evaluate it as an optional cached world-generation stage, including its computational cost and license.
- [Real-time Terrain Enhancement with Controlled Procedural Patterns](https://diglib.eg.org/items/43319e3d-ba25-4762-971b-ac838ec21fb8) (Grenier et al., 2024) uses terrain slope to orient cascaded ravine patterns. **Applied here:** the general principle of slope-guided detail, using our own analytic value-noise gradients and directional filtering. This is not their phasor-noise algorithm; no paper code is included. The paper is published under a non-commercial license.
- [Terrain Amplification using Multi-Scale Erosion](https://h-schott.github.io/p/mserosion/) (Schott et al., 2024) combines erosion and deposition processes across scales to maintain hydrological structure. **Applied here:** cached coarse world drainage plus independent procedural detail. A regional water and sediment solver is retained for experiments but does not change the displayed terrain. The paper’s complete multi-scale erosion method is not implemented; fine detail is not hydrologically guaranteed.
- [Fast Hydraulic Erosion Simulation and Visualization on GPU](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4392715/) (Mei, Decaudin and Hu, 2007) describes a GPU shallow-water approach to erosion and sediment transport. **Applied here:** the general idea of water-driven transport in a small independent regional solver, using our own four-neighbor flux and artist-tuned coefficients. This is not a reproduction of the paper’s solver.
- [FastFlow: GPU Acceleration of Flow and Depression Routing for Landscape Simulation](https://diglib.eg.org/items/5c046b92-a474-429d-9d0f-c028b55eef36) (Jain et al., 2024) motivates GPU flow accumulation and depression routing. **Applied here:** a separate, simpler fixed-iteration relaxation and gather pass; this is not FastFlow and does not inherit its convergence or performance guarantees.
- [r.watershed](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass85/manuals/r.watershed.html) documents single- and multiple-flow-direction watershed analysis. **Applied here:** one downhill neighbor per node on a depression-filled surface; unlike a full watershed model, the result is a coarse rendering approximation.
- [Generating Complex Procedural Terrains Using the GPU](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu) (NVIDIA GPU Gems 3) describes multi-scale procedural fields and domain warping. **Applied here:** low-frequency 3D domain warp and bounded higher-frequency relief bands. The planet is a heightfield, not a volumetric cave system.

## Planet-scale rendering

- [Geometry Clipmaps](https://hhoppe.com/proj/geomclipmap/) (Losasso and Hoppe, 2004) and [GPU-based Geometry Clipmaps](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-i-geometric-complexity/chapter-2-terrain-rendering-using-gpu-based-geometry) demonstrate nested grids and incremental GPU terrain updates. **Applied here:** reusable regular grids and GPU-side terrain generation; this lab uses a cube-sphere quadtree rather than a clipmap.
- [3D Tiles selection and screen-space error](https://cesium.com/learn/cesium-native/ref-doc/selection-algorithm-details.html) shows refinement based on estimated geometric error. **Current implementation:** measured parent/child differences contribute to a projected-error priority, alongside a grid-cell-size floor and a hard patch budget. The measurements are not conservative bounds and do not guarantee a maximum screen-space error.
- [Horizon culling](https://cesium.com/blog/2013/04/25/horizon-culling/) discusses avoiding false culls when terrain crosses an idealized ellipsoid. **Applied here:** CPU-side conservative horizon rejection uses an inner and outer radius with a margin for bounded displacement.

## Implemented pipeline

1. Six consistently oriented cube faces form a sphere with an Earth-like mean radius of 6,371 km. A CPU quadtree selects patches within a user-controlled budget; the depth can reach 20 in orbit and 22 while walking when the budget allows. Adjacent leaves are balanced to a maximum level difference of one, including across cube faces. Measured differences between parent and child patches feed the next selection's projected-error priority; unsampled areas use a projected grid-cell-size floor. A priority margin on previously split nodes reduces churn. These measurements are not conservative bounds.
2. Each 16 × 16-cell patch has 16 meshlets of 4 × 4 cells, 32 triangles per meshlet. Coarse-neighbor edge flags collapse odd edge vertices to match the adjacent grid. Replacements are generated in batches in spare GPU slots while the existing patches remain visible. A GPU comparison samples the actual two meshes, including collapsed edges, and projects the difference using the current camera. The new selection replaces the old one in a single frame. If a coarsening exceeds the 0.5-pixel target, the renderer retains the old mesh where coverage permits and measures again as the view changes. Refinement and newly uncovered areas can exceed the target so the view is not left incomplete. If a selection includes both refinement and a merge above target, it may switch after 1.2 seconds to avoid blocking detail indefinitely. The reported maximum is a sampled difference, not a guarantee of zero visible popping.
3. Seeded plate centers, warped continental fields, ridged multifractals, directional folds and fine relief bands form the base terrain. Analytic gradients of larger ridges orient two scales of incised valley detail. Seeded bands displace vertices from tens of meters down to sub-meter scales. Four nested, world-space channel fields use interpolated coarse drainage direction, downhill slope and discharge to add drainage-oriented incisions, with high/low precision and frequencies chosen to keep shared edges stable. The directional influence fades near conflicting flow directions. These channels are an independently written procedural approximation; there is no plate-motion simulation or photographic height map.
4. Once per seed, a separate GPU pass builds a world field on six 64 × 64 cube faces. The 24,578 vertices at their shared edges and corners use the same simulation data. On this coarse grid, fixed-iteration minimax relaxation approximates depression filling, one downhill neighbor is selected for each land node, and rainfall accumulates through the graph. Three passes use an artist-tuned stream-power-style rule to remove material and transport and deposit sediment. After routing, 128 gather iterations propagate the lowest bed elevation through each connected depression sharing a spill level; that floor supports a common bounded lake surface. The final graph, filled elevations, basin floors and erosion delta are cached. Changing the erosion slider scales that cached delta; it does not rerun the simulation for each slider position.
5. The displayed height field uses a seeded, world-space drainage approximation independently of the camera and regional cache. This avoids moving terrain when a cache tile loads or is evicted. **Regional drainage detail** controls the additional procedural incision.
6. A separate experimental regional GPU solver is retained for the regional smoke test. Its fixed tiles use 64 × 64 interiors with halos, four-neighbor water transport and six erosion/deposition iterations; the solver's output is not applied to the displayed mesh.
7. GPU-generated river splines join the coarse drainage graph nodes, with seeded broad bends strongest on flatter terrain. A denser coverage field is cached per terrain patch for channel carving, while visible water is evaluated at each fragment's geographic direction, independent of the current patch or LOD. Land and sea use the same seeded terrain field at every LOD; conservative bounds avoid evaluating all terrain detail at unambiguous locations. A bounded, smooth reduction of ridge detail near sea level produces less fragmented coasts while preserving higher coastal relief. The normal view renders the ocean independently by intersecting each view ray with a sea-level sphere and checking the seeded geographic coast; ocean color uses the continuous broad continental field as an inexpensive bed-depth approximation. Narrow rivers use footprint-based filtering as they become subpixel. Lake coverage is localized around supported coarse filled depressions; a broader terrain field determines shorelines. Where the terrain pass identifies a lake and the basin intersection is valid, a second pass renders the basin-level sphere. The terrain-colored edge remains as a fallback. The mask comes from the seed's global field rather than the regional cache, so region loading does not create or remove lakes. To avoid spurious seas from interpolated erosion, already-dry land stays above sea level. Moisture- and temperature-inspired color uses runoff, rain, latitude and elevation; slope-dependent snow and highlight compression preserve highland contrast. These effects are analytical and use no image textures.
8. Compute passes calculate fixed-world fine and broad terrain slopes, then filter between them according to pixel footprint. These normals no longer depend on neighboring patch LODs; approximate mesh-based cavity shading has been removed to avoid resolution-dependent changes. A compute pass frustum-culls active meshlets and writes an indirect draw. Camera-relative positions, reversed-Z depth and high/low spherical patch centers improve large-world precision. Navigation searches and a small surface probe run on the GPU.

## Current limitations

- The world field has spacing on the order of 100 km, uses one flow direction per node and fixed iteration counts, and is not a calibrated physical simulation. Regional tiles are a six-step, four-neighbor approximation, with coarse runoff used as an initial water input; they do not exchange a full river-network flow boundary condition across the whole planet. Fine channels and the coarse river spline are not guaranteed to follow a monotonically descending regional terrain profile. Some seeds or more complex terrain could exceed the fixed iteration limits, including the connected-basin floor propagation. Regional water is evaporated and replenished by rainfall; erosion and deposition are capped for stability, and independently cropped and blended regions are not a globally mass-conserving watershed. Lakes are a filled-surface approximation; the displayed water is not a dynamic fluid surface.
- Climate colors are an artistic approximation, not a climate or ecosystem simulation. Rock materials, cast terrain shadows, clouds, atmospheric scattering and volumetric caves are absent; this is not photorealistic Earth.
- The quadtree combines a projected grid-cell-size floor with asynchronously measured parent/child differences. The 0.5-pixel transition threshold applies to sampled coarsening when old coverage can be kept; refinement, new coverage and fast camera changes can exceed it. Finite samples, budget and edge stitching also prevent a guarantee of zero popping or seams. Ocean intersections use an independent sphere, but land occlusion, lake coverage and shore edges still depend partly on sampled terrain and can move with LOD.
- Shared-edge GPU tests show sub-meter differences on coarse planet-scale patches on the test adapter; tests also exercise erosion-shaped land across a cube-face edge at close range and after camera movement. High/low sampling improves the smallest noise bands, but larger-scale fields and GPU implementation differences still limit precision; centimeter-scale guarantees need further work.
- Fine procedural bands are not fully frequency-filtered by projected sampling rate and can alias during descent. Stable band-limited residuals are future work.
- Patch selection runs on the CPU and is recomputed as the view changes. Staging reserves a second set of geometry slots, and fixed-world normals require extra terrain evaluations during patch generation; a separate water pass and depth target add GPU work and memory. Changing the seed requires world regeneration. Geographic classification and water shading evaluate terrain or river segments per fragment. The regional solver buffers remain allocated for its tests even though it is disabled for the visible terrain. Real hardware should be profiled before making performance claims.
